How to audit a management action log before the weekly review

A useful weekly action review begins before the meeting opens. Someone checks whether each recorded commitment has enough information to discuss intelligently: a recognizable task, an accountable owner, an interpretable date, and a reason to believe a closed item is actually complete. When those fields are missing, the meeting often becomes an exercise in reconstructing last week rather than making this week's decisions.

This guide explains a small, local audit you can run on a CSV export. The accompanying Python utility reads the file and produces a review report. It does not assign work, assess employee performance, change deadlines, or send messages. Its purpose is narrower and testable: find records that need a person's attention before the team relies on the log. The worked examples are fictional and do not describe results achieved by any company.

The process is useful even if you never run the program. A spreadsheet filter can expose the same gaps. The value comes from a shared definition of a usable commitment, a consistent review date, and a clear distinction between correcting a record and deciding what the business should do. Automation makes that agreement repeatable; it cannot supply the missing judgment.

What this audit adds to a delegation system

Delegation defines who can decide and when an exception needs escalation. An action log records the particular commitments that follow. These are related tasks, but they fail in different ways. A sound delegation policy can coexist with a poor log in which three people think someone else owns the next step. Conversely, a tidy log can document a decision that its owner never had authority to make.

Use an exception log when the question is whether a decision belongs with the team or the owner. Use this audit when the question is whether a specific recorded action can be reviewed without guesswork. For example, “choose a replacement supplier” may be a legitimate delegated decision. The action record still needs a named coordinator, a date, and an agreed description of the deliverable. Passing the audit does not validate the supplier choice.

Keep the audit outside individual performance ratings. A past-due record could reflect an obsolete date, a dependency, a changed priority, or an unresolved customer request. None of those explanations appears automatically in the export. The report should open a conversation about the commitment, not become a league table of supposedly productive employees. Decisions about capacity and accountability need context that this utility deliberately does not collect.

Define a small input contract

The download expects exactly six columns in this order: action_id, action, owner, status, due_date, and closure_evidence. Use the template rather than guessing column names. Exact headers make an accidental export from another system visible immediately. A file containing extra fields is rejected instead of quietly discarding information that a user may assume was reviewed. This is a deliberately small example, not a universal project-management interchange format.

An action identifier should remain stable when the wording changes. Give “confirm the revised delivery window” an identifier such as A104, then keep that identifier through subsequent reviews. Two records with the same identifier are flagged. The utility cannot tell whether the duplication is an accidental copy or a legitimate attempt to split work. A person must resolve the ambiguity by merging the records or assigning distinct identifiers with an explanation.

Write the action as an observable next step. “Customer experience” is a theme, while “send the customer the approved installation checklist” describes work someone can verify. The audit only checks that the action field is present; it does not grade the quality of the sentence. During the first few reviews, read several passing records aloud. If colleagues still ask what completion means, improve the wording before expanding the automation.

Choose statuses people can interpret consistently

This example accepts open, closed, and cancelled. The vocabulary is intentionally limited. An open item represents a commitment still being managed. A closed item represents work the team regards as complete. A cancelled item represents a decision to stop pursuing the commitment. Closure evidence is required for both closed and cancelled records, because disappearance from the active list should have an understandable explanation.

Evidence can be a document reference, a ticket number, a dated acceptance note, or the decision that cancelled the work. It need not be a publicly accessible link. “Closed because obsolete after approved scope change SC12” is more informative than “done.” The script checks that the field contains text. It cannot open a reference or establish that the evidence supports the stated result. That remains a review responsibility.

If your system uses blocked, deferred, or in progress, decide how to map those states before exporting. Do not let an AI invent the mapping each week. You might keep them as open in this teaching format and retain the richer state in your source system. Document the transformation and preserve the original export. If the extra states matter to the review, extend the tool and its tests rather than squeezing away important meaning.

Use an explicit review date

Run the audit with an as-of date in YYYY-MM-DD format. An explicit date makes a report reproducible. A colleague rerunning the same file with the same date should receive the same findings even if they work in another time zone or open the file next month. The tool rejects ambiguous forms such as 11/04/2026 rather than guessing whether the author intended April or November.

Only open actions dated before the supplied review date are marked past due. An action due on the review date is not marked past due by this rule. This is a date comparison, not a statement about an employment agreement, customer promise, contractual cutoff, or the end of a workday. If your operation requires an exact time, this date-only tool is too limited and should not be used to imply that precision.

Missing dates are also findings for open items. Sometimes a team consciously leaves an action unscheduled while waiting for a dependency. That may be a reasonable operating choice, but it should be discussed explicitly. Do not insert a convenient Friday date solely to make the report green. A better response is to clarify the dependency, identify who will obtain the missing information, and decide how the unresolved commitment will appear in the next review.

Prepare the export without losing the source

Save a dated copy of the export before making corrections. Work on a second file with the agreed six columns. This separates what the system actually contained from the version prepared for review. Keep sensitive employee, customer, and commercial details out of a demonstration file. The fictional sample is sufficient to learn the workflow, so there is no need to paste a live management log into an external AI service.

CSV records can contain commas, quotation marks, and line breaks inside a field. Use a spreadsheet export or a proper CSV writer rather than building rows by joining text with commas. The utility uses Python's csv reader with UTF-8 input and accepts a UTF-8 byte-order mark. The Python documentation explains the reader and newline handling; the business rules in this guide are our own example conventions.

A record number in the report refers to the parsed CSV record, with the header counted first. A quoted multiline action can occupy more than one physical text line. Therefore, record 4 is not necessarily physical line 4 in a text editor. Locate a finding by its action identifier and confirm the source record before editing. This small distinction matters when a colleague uses a multiline note and someone else reviews the raw file.

Run the local utility

Download action_log_audit.py and the fictional CSV sample into a folder you control. Use an installed Python 3 interpreter and run: python3 action_log_audit.py fictional-actions.csv --as-of 2026-10-25. The program prints JSON to standard output. To save a report, redirect that output to a new file you choose. It does not edit the CSV, contact a server, install packages, or create assignments.

A structural input problem produces an Input error message and exit code 2. Examples include missing headers, extra columns, a header-only file, or a record with the wrong number of fields. Those errors mean the report could not be produced reliably. Correct the export contract before interpreting business findings. Do not treat the absence of a JSON report as evidence that the log had no problems.

A successfully parsed report can still contain findings. The program returns normally when it has inspected records, even if some have missing owners or past-due dates. This distinction separates an unusable input file from a usable file containing unresolved work. A dashboard should preserve that distinction. If you later integrate the tool into a build or scheduled check, test the policy you want rather than assuming every finding should stop a release.

Read the fictional example carefully

Suppose A1 says “Ask José, then confirm the delivery window,” has no owner, remains open, and was due October 24. With an October 25 review date, the report flags missing_owner and past_due. Neither finding tells you whom to assign. The appropriate next step is to ask the manager responsible for that workflow who accepted the commitment and whether the original date remains meaningful.

A2 records an approved shipment, names Jo, is closed, and points to receipt 72. That row passes the mechanical rules. A reviewer should still sample the receipt if shipment completion is material to a decision. A passing row means required fields are present and interpretable under the input contract. It does not prove the shipment arrived, the customer accepted it, or the underlying data is accurate.

A3 says “close job,” is marked closed, and has no evidence. The report flags missing_closure_evidence. An unhelpful correction would be typing “complete” into the evidence column. A useful correction would identify the acceptance record or acknowledge that the job was marked closed prematurely. If the team chooses to reopen it, preserve the reason in the source system so the next reviewer understands the change.

A fourth record reuses A1 and supplies a date written as 10/25/2026. It receives duplicate_id and invalid_due_date. Resolve both issues independently. Changing the date format does not resolve the identifier conflict, and assigning a new identifier does not establish what the original author meant by the date. Multiple findings on one row are an invitation to inspect the record, not a request to make arbitrary edits until the warnings disappear.

Turn findings into a short review agenda

Group the report by the kind of decision required. Missing owners call for confirmation of responsibility. Date problems call for clarification of timing or dependencies. Closure-evidence gaps call for acceptance or cancellation support. Duplicate identifiers call for source cleanup. The people needed to resolve those groups may differ, so send the relevant records to the appropriate review owner instead of broadcasting the entire log.

For each group, prepare a concise question. “Who accepted A1?” is more useful than “Please fix the action log.” “Which source establishes completion of A3?” directs attention toward evidence. Keep the question neutral when the cause is unknown. The purpose of preflight review is to reduce ambiguity before the meeting, not to frame a person as responsible for an error that may have been introduced during export.

After the meeting, update the authoritative system and produce a fresh export. Rerun against the same review date if you are checking the corrections from that review. Save a brief explanation of material changes, especially a cancelled commitment or a moved deadline. A clean second report is helpful, but the decision trail explains why it became clean and prevents a tidy spreadsheet from replacing the actual operating conversation.

Handle exceptions without corrupting the log

An action may depend on a third party who has not provided a date. The owner can still be known even when completion timing is unresolved. Keep those two questions separate. You may need an additional follow-up action such as “request the delivery estimate,” with its own owner and date. That does not justify inventing a completion date for the original work or pretending the dependency has disappeared.

A group-owned task may also need a coordinator. “Operations” can be a meaningful department but an unclear review contact. The script accepts that text because it cannot evaluate your organizational structure. Your review convention should say whether the owner field names a person, a role, or a team mailbox, and who answers when the commitment changes. Choose the convention that makes the handoff operationally clear.

Recurring work needs a deliberate identifier policy. If each weekly inspection is a distinct commitment, use a distinct occurrence identifier while retaining a separate recurring-process reference in the source system. Reusing one ID across several open occurrences makes it difficult to tell what is late. The teaching CSV has no recurrence engine. Adding one requires explicit rules for holidays, exceptions, cancelled occurrences, and what happens when a previous instance remains unfinished.

Where AI can help and where it needs review

An AI assistant can propose clearer action wording from approved notes, suggest possible duplicate descriptions, or draft neutral questions for missing fields. Give it a constrained output format and ask it to preserve identifiers and source references. Keep suggested edits separate from the authoritative log until someone checks them. A fluent rewrite can accidentally turn “consider a vendor” into “select a vendor,” changing the commitment rather than clarifying it.

Do not ask an assistant to infer an owner from who spoke most often in a transcript. Participation is not acceptance of responsibility. Similarly, a phrase such as “we should do that soon” does not establish an approved deadline. Preserve unresolved information as unresolved. A useful prompt asks the assistant to list the supporting passage and mark any proposed interpretation for review instead of silently filling the record.

For broader implementation planning, connect this narrow audit to a documented delegation and approval workflow. Decide who may approve corrected actions, where revisions are stored, and what information may enter an AI system. Elixir's operating-system resources can help frame that organizational work. This local utility demonstrates a record check; it does not implement your company's access controls, document retention policy, or approval authority.

Frequently asked questions

Does a zero-finding report mean the action log is accurate?

No. It means the records passed these particular mechanical checks. An owner could be named incorrectly, evidence could refer to the wrong job, or an action could be so vague that it cannot be verified. Review a sample of passing records against the source. Increase scrutiny when a commitment affects a customer, a material expense, or a decision that is difficult to reverse.

Should every past-due action be escalated to the owner?

Use your agreed escalation rules. The report supplies a date finding, not an escalation policy. A team lead may be able to resolve a dependency without owner involvement. An apparently small delay may also affect a critical commitment. Review impact and authority alongside the date, and record the next decision in the source system rather than treating the audit as a substitute for management.

Can I rename columns to match my project software?

You can modify the program, but document the change and test it with an actual export. The supplied version deliberately requires exact headers. A small transformation step is often easier to review than making the validator accept many loosely defined alternatives. Preserve the original export so you can tell whether a missing value came from the source system or from your transformation.

Why does a cancelled item need closure evidence?

Cancellation changes what the team expects to happen. A reference to the decision prevents someone from restarting obsolete work or wondering why a commitment vanished. The evidence can be a short decision note, not a lengthy report. If cancellation came from a scope change, point to that change so the action log and the operating plan continue to describe the same reality.

What should I do with confidential evidence references?

Keep the underlying material in the approved system and use a reference appropriate for the audience receiving the log. A report does not need to contain the full customer document or employee discussion. Review access before sharing it. Local execution reduces unnecessary transmission, but files, backups, screenshots, and exported reports still need the same handling discipline as other business records.

Can this become an automated weekly process?

Yes, after the team has used it manually and agreed how findings will be handled. Start with a review report rather than automatic changes. Test malformed exports, new status values, duplicate records, and the absence of data. Assign someone to maintain the input contract when your project software changes. A recurring process is only useful if a person notices when its assumptions stop matching the source.

A practical first review

Begin with one team's current commitments and a fictional practice run. Confirm the export format, choose a review date, and inspect both flagged and passing records. Resolve a small number of findings in the authoritative system, then rerun and compare the result. Record what the audit missed. That feedback is more valuable than adding dozens of checks before anyone has seen how the first six fields behave in real work.

Keep the review proportional. The objective is a reliable conversation about commitments, supported by traceable records and explicit decisions. When the tool exposes a gap, use the gap to improve the operating agreement. When the tool passes a row, retain ordinary professional judgment. A compact audit earns its place when it helps a team prepare, clarify, and follow through without creating another disconnected version of the work.

How should we retire old audit reports?

Decide a retention convention with the people responsible for your business records. Keep enough history to explain material changes without scattering sensitive exports across personal folders. Name reports with the input version and review date, and distinguish a superseded report from the current agenda. The program does not manage retention or delete anything. If you later automate cleanup, confirm that the process preserves the authoritative source and any records your organization must retain. Test cleanup on fictional files before applying it to a real management archive.

An example of a useful correction note

Suppose the review confirms that A1 belongs to the dispatch coordinator and that the original date referred to requesting information, not completing delivery. Record that distinction in the source: the information request is one action, while confirming delivery may be a separate commitment after the response arrives. Do not simply move the original date and leave its meaning unclear. If the work is split, assign distinct identifiers and retain the relationship between them.

At the following review, inspect whether the corrected records made the conversation clearer. The audit can show that the fields now pass, but the team should judge whether the handoff became understandable. That gives you a practical improvement criterion without claiming that fewer warnings automatically mean better business performance. Sometimes clearer records reveal more unresolved work, which is useful information rather than evidence that the audit failed.
