The decision this workflow supports

Use this guide to align promised work with available resources. The finished deliverable is a capacity table and schedule decisions. For staffing agencies, the basic unit of work is a candidate placement. Keeping that unit visible prevents a broad business summary from hiding the specific action, commitment or source record that needs review.

A profile submission, interview, offer and started placement are separate stages. Use only authorized candidate information and objective job criteria. The recruiting manager should confirm how this distinction applies to the current task. Choose a single period, project or decision before supplying information to an AI system. A narrowly defined question makes it easier to verify the resulting draft and to identify what the model cannot establish from the available evidence.

Gather the right source records

Begin with open work, task duration estimates, staff schedules and dependency statuses. In this business context, relevant operating evidence may come from approved job brief, candidate-authorized profile, interview record and client feedback. Select only the records necessary for the task and use a system your team has approved for that information. Replace unnecessary personal details with internal references where possible.

A worked operating scenario

A client interviews three candidates and makes one offer. The candidate has not accepted or started, so the placement should remain in the offer stage.

Apply this task to that situation by preparing a capacity table and schedule decisions. The review should answer: What job criteria are approved? What stage is confirmed? Who owns the next action? A useful draft states which part of the situation is confirmed, which part remains an assumption and what the recruiting manager needs before approving the next action.

For comparison, consider the task-specific pattern: A team has forty labor hours available but only one specialist can complete the final review. General availability does not remove that specialist bottleneck. This pattern is a method example, not an assertion about the current business. Use it to check whether the draft preserves the same distinction in the supplied candidate placement records.

Build the working file in five steps

  1. Define the scope. Write the decision to align promised work with available resources, the selected period or item and the person who can approve the outcome.
  2. Prepare the evidence. Collect approved job brief, candidate-authorized profile, interview record and client feedback as relevant to the task. Label confirmed records, working estimates and missing inputs separately.
  3. Apply the method. Separate committed work from tentative requests. Estimate capacity by the actual constraint, then identify work that requires a scheduling decision.
  4. Review the business distinction. Check the draft against this requirement: A stage update must be supported by a recorded event and must not infer acceptance.
  5. Close the handoff. Have the recruiting manager review the deliverable, record the accepted version and assign an owner and date to each unresolved item.

Acceptance and review boundaries

Historical duration is an estimate until the current scope and dependencies are confirmed. In staffing agencies, also check that a stage update must be supported by a recorded event and must not infer acceptance. These are two separate reviews: one protects the task boundary and the other checks the industry-specific operating record. Both should be visible in the final file.

If the draft includes arithmetic, use reproducible worksheet formulas and have the appropriate finance owner review the inputs. If the task touches a legal document, technical property condition, lending term or regulated decision, route that part to the qualified professional responsible for it. The AI draft organizes work; it does not establish professional conclusions or authorize a business commitment.

Measure the workflow after use

Track completed commitments within the approved capacity plan. Define the numerator, denominator and reporting period before comparing results. Include preparation and correction time when judging whether the workflow helps. A first pilot can be considered useful when the reviewer can trace its findings, accept the deliverable and identify the next action without reconstructing the source history.

Review a small set of completed tasks with the recruiting manager. Record recurring corrections and improve either the source register, prompt or checklist. Keep changes versioned. The aim is a reliable operating habit for a candidate placement, rather than a single impressive answer that cannot be checked later.

Practical questions

What should the AI produce for this task?

Ask for a capacity table and schedule decisions, using separate committed work from tentative requests. Estimate capacity by the actual constraint, then identify work that requires a scheduling decision. Keep the candidate placement reference, evidence status and review owner visible. The final result should answer the defined decision rather than expanding into unrelated recommendations.

What if the source records are incomplete?

Mark the missing field and explain which conclusion it prevents. For this context, ask: What job criteria are approved? What stage is confirmed? Who owns the next action? Assign the evidence request before treating an assumption as a verified finding.

Who should approve the result?

The recruiting manager or the person designated by the business authority table should approve the operating result. Historical duration is an estimate until the current scope and dependencies are confirmed. Specialist conclusions remain with the qualified reviewer responsible for them.
