Review a Role Scorecard Against Scope, Measures and Support

By Built to Run Editorial · Published July 11, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Read the role document as a proposal with a version

A role scorecard can organize a conversation about responsibilities and evidence. It does not establish why a hiring difficulty occurred or guarantee that a person will work independently. Identify the document version, intended role and review period before using it to discuss an assignment.

Separate what is proposed from what has been accepted. A draft can name responsibilities without showing that the person has agreed to them, has access to the needed information or can make the relevant decisions. Record those questions beside the proposed responsibility.

Make each measure interpretable

For each proposed measure, ask what is counted, which records supply the count, which work is included and what period it covers. A response-time measure needs a defined start and qualifying response; a completion count needs an identified completion condition. A percentage needs its numerator and denominator. A label alone does not supply those definitions.

Keep a target separate from an observation. A proposed threshold is not evidence that it is appropriate, attainable or accepted for this role. If records are missing, mark the observation as incomplete rather than entering a zero or assuming success. Changes to a measure definition can also change the comparison even when the label stays the same.

Connect responsibility to support and boundaries

Ask which decisions are included, which require review and which remain unresolved. Identify the information, access and handoffs the proposed work depends on. Naming a person as an owner does not by itself establish authority over another team's work or the availability of a required input.

Describe an exclusion directly. For example, preparing a draft and sending an approved document are separate actions. If sending is outside the proposed role, keep it outside the scorecard's assessment unless the scope is explicitly changed and accepted. A proposed change should remain visible as a proposal.

Keep an example distinct from employee evidence

In a fictional example, Mira's draft scorecard says “prepare the weekly summary.” Owen's review note asks for a source record that Mira has not received. The record supports a question about the missing input and the scope of preparation. It does not establish poor performance, unwillingness, an unsuitable hire or a completed handoff.

A useful review note would identify the scorecard version, requested summary, missing record and next question. It would keep any later observation separate from the earlier draft. The example supplies no actual employee assessment or recommendation about a real hiring decision.

Prepare the next review conversation

Bring the current document and the records already available. Ask which expectations are understood, which measures remain undefined, which dependencies need clarification and which proposed changes require acceptance. Preserve unresolved questions instead of turning a blank field into a performance conclusion.

This article offers original document-review questions. No actual scorecard, employee, hiring process or book passage was inspected. It establishes no candidate quality, professional assessment, accepted assignment, hiring benefit or operating outcome.

Common questions

Does a role scorecard prove someone can work without support?

No. A document describes expectations. Record what support was available and what was observed separately from a proposed assessment.

Are example targets suitable for every role?

No. Identify the actual measure, population, period and agreed scope before considering a target.

Does a named responsibility establish decision authority?

A proposed responsibility does not establish accepted authority. Ask which decisions, approvals and exceptions are included.

Can a missed measure identify the cause of a problem?

The measure alone does not establish cause. Check the underlying records, task scope, dependencies and unresolved questions.
