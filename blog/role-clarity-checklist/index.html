Agree the work and its boundaries

Role Clarity Checklist: Outcomes, Authority, and Handoffs

Define the outcome, decisions, dependencies, and handoffs before treating a role as clear.

Published September 23, 2026Reviewed November 22, 2026By Dr. Connor Robertson

A role title does not tell someone which decision they may make, which result they own, or what to do when two managers give incompatible instructions. Use this checklist to agree a small, testable role charter with the person doing the work and the people who depend on it.

Start with one recurring outcome, such as preparing a service schedule or reviewing an invoice exception. Keep the organization's existing permissions, contractual requirements, and approval policies in force. Writing a name in a worksheet does not grant that person authority.

Agree eight parts of the role

Write observable boundaries rather than broad expectations such as “take ownership.” Have the receiving person explain the agreement back using a recent task. A disagreement is an item to resolve, not a failed employee score.

Three fictional boundary problems

These invented situations illustrate how to fill the charter. They are not client cases, measured outcomes, or recommended authority limits.

A scheduler receives a request for additional work

The role coordinates the agreed service schedule. Its existing authority permits moving an appointment within the approved schedule, but does not permit changing scope or price. A customer asks for another service during the same visit. The scheduler records the request and sends it to the authorized reviewer. The charter names who communicates the decision; the original appointment status remains separate from the unapproved addition.

An invoice coordinator finds conflicting records

The role gathers the invoice and supporting records for review. It cannot change bank instructions or authorize payment. A document conflicts with the approved record. The coordinator flags the discrepancy through the established verification process and keeps it unresolved until the authorized reviewer responds. “Own the invoice process” does not mean bypassing separation of duties.

A backup has time but lacks access

A report owner will be absent. The proposed backup has capacity but cannot access a required system under their own account. Naming that person as backup does not make coverage ready. The manager arranges the appropriate access through the normal process or changes the coverage plan. The charter records the dependency and which work must wait; it does not recommend sharing credentials.

Test one work cycle before expanding the role

  1. Choose a normal task and a foreseeable exception within the agreed scope. Use a rehearsal if acting on a live task would create an inappropriate risk.
  2. Ask the person to identify the outcome, permitted decision, required input, next recipient, and escalation condition using the current records.
  3. Ask the receiving team what information it needs. Resolve a disagreement about “done” before treating the handoff as accepted.
  4. Record a missing permission, conflicting priority, unclear boundary, or unavailable backup as an open dependency with a named reviewer.
  5. Review the cycle together. Update the agreement through the authorized process and keep the superseded version distinguishable.

Observe specific events such as a handoff returned for missing information or a decision escalated because the boundary was unclear. Record the observation period and task context. Do not turn a completed worksheet into proof of competence, sufficient staffing, or improved performance.

Resolve conflicting instructions explicitly

When two requests exceed available capacity or point in different directions, make the conflict visible to the person who can resolve it. Record the requests, relevant deadlines, consequence of delay, and the decision needed. The role holder should know whom to contact and what interim action is permitted under the existing process.

Do not silently make both people accountable for an unresolved decision. Several people may contribute work, and an organization can have shared duties. For this worksheet, identify the coordinating owner and the authorized decision maker for the particular outcome. That is a practical documentation choice, not a universal rule about legal responsibility.

Questions before the charter is agreed

Does this replace the job description?

No. Use it to clarify a defined workflow alongside the job description, reporting relationships, and applicable policies. Resolve inconsistencies with the responsible manager rather than assuming the worksheet overrides them.

Can one person hold several roles?

Yes, where the arrangement fits capacity and the organization's controls. Record which role is acting in each decision and preserve any required independent review.

When should the role be reviewed?

Set a review point appropriate to the work and revisit it when the scope, systems, staffing, permissions, or key dependencies change. An agreed review date does not make a known conflict safe to ignore until then.

Source and scope

The UK Health and Safety Executive's Management Standards guidance on role emphasizes understandable responsibilities, compatible requirements, and ways to raise uncertainty or conflict. That supports the narrow background here. The charter, checklist, and fictional examples are original teaching tools; they are not an HSE assessment, endorsement, or legal compliance determination.
