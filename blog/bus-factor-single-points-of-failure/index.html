Questions to Check a Proposed Backup for Critical Work

By Built to Run Editorial · Published September 5, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

A backup plan can contain a person's name while leaving the actual work unresolved. This article offers preparation questions for reviewing one proposed handover. It does not assess an employee's competence, certify continuity or establish that a business can operate through someone's absence.

Name the work that needs coverage

Describe a specific task rather than a whole department. “Prepare the weekly delivery schedule from these accepted orders” is a narrower question than “cover operations.” Identify the input, expected output and point at which the task is considered complete. If the usual worker makes exceptions, record which decisions remain outside the proposed backup's role.

Ask what would happen if this particular task were delayed. Keep an observed consequence separate from a possible one. A missed internal draft date and an unfulfilled customer commitment are different records. Do not assume that the function associated with the most memorable person is automatically the highest priority.

Distinguish a proposed person from an accepted role

Has the named person agreed to this work, and what other work would they be doing during the same period? A manager's intention to assign coverage is different from an accepted assignment. Write down who is expected to confirm the arrangement and leave the status open until that response exists.

Clarify the boundaries of the role. Preparing a draft, reviewing a draft and authorizing its use are separate actions. A person who has agreed to prepare a schedule has not necessarily agreed to approve changes to customer commitments. An escalation contact should be identified as a proposed contact until that person has accepted the responsibility.

Check prerequisites without assuming access

List the records, tools and instructions the task uses. Ask whether the proposed backup has the appropriate access through the organization's established process. Knowing where a file lives does not establish permission to open or change it. A plan should not depend on sharing another person's credentials or borrowing their identity.

Keep instructions specific to the version and task reviewed. If a written step refers to a different system or omits an exception, record the gap. Do not treat the existence of a document as evidence that another person has read it or can complete the work described.

Describe practice by what was actually observed

A practice record can identify the task, supplied inputs, conditions, assistance and resulting output. If the primary worker answered questions throughout, retain that fact. Assisted practice is evidence about that assisted attempt; it does not establish how an unsupported attempt would go.

Choose any exercise through the appropriate operational review and retain normal approvals. This article does not recommend making a critical worker unavailable for a week or withholding help. A completed practice task also leaves other tasks, unusual conditions and future availability unresolved.

Record the remaining question

For an original fictional example, suppose Mira agrees to prepare a draft weekly schedule using an existing template. The acceptance record does not say who approves exceptions, and the practice attempt used sample orders with no changes. Record those two gaps directly. Do not describe the team as fully covered or infer that Mira failed at an unattempted task.

A next step might be asking the existing approver to clarify the exception route. That is a proposal until accepted. Keep the task, role, prerequisites, practice conditions and unanswered questions together so a later reviewer can see what the plan actually establishes.

Common questions

Does naming a backup establish readiness?

No. The name alone does not establish acceptance, access, capacity or performance on a particular task.

Is one successful practice attempt enough to certify coverage?

This article provides no certification threshold. Describe the task and conditions observed and keep untested conditions open.

Should assistance during practice be omitted?

No. Record the assistance so the result is not represented as an unsupported attempt.

No. These are original preparation questions, not a quotation or a claim about a specific chapter or complete book method.
