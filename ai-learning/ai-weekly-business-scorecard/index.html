Build an AI-Assisted Weekly Business Scorecard

Prepare an operating review with defined metrics, reliable sources and clear follow-through.

Original website companion resource. Published October 9, 2026. This guide is not an excerpt or a claim about the original book chapters.

Choose metrics that change decisions

A weekly scorecard should help managers act. Select a small set of measures covering demand, delivery, cash and capacity. Examples include qualified appointments, completed work, overdue receivables and open items awaiting approval. Choose metrics appropriate to the business. AI can organize candidate metrics, but the leadership team must decide which measures connect to current operating constraints.

Create a metric dictionary

Define each metric, counting rule, source system, reporting cutoff, responsible person and target. Funded sales and signed agreements measure different events. Completed work should have a completion definition. Revenue and cash collected should remain separate. Give the model this dictionary so it summarizes the same measure each week. Without definitions, a polished report can hide inconsistent counting.

An illustrative operating review

A team completes 45 jobs against a weekly target of 50. Its report should identify the five-job gap, the number awaiting client input and the number awaiting internal review. If four jobs are waiting on approval, the next action may be to assign review capacity rather than increase sales. AI can summarize that pattern after the source records and category counts are verified.

A prompt for meeting preparation

Use: Prepare a weekly scorecard narrative from this metric dictionary and approved data. Show actual, target, difference and prior period. Cite the source for each actual. Identify the three largest actionable gaps and draft questions for the responsible managers. Do not infer causes from correlation. Keep missing data separate from missed targets. End with a table for decisions, owners and dates.

Close the loop after the meeting

Record actions against the metric they should influence. Review whether last week actions were completed and whether the underlying measure changed. AI can draft minutes and compare periods, but the meeting owner validates the record. This practice makes the scorecard an operating instrument. It reduces the need for the founder to personally assemble every update and interpret every team request.
